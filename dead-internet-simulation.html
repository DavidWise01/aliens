Ordinary convergence

Finite vocabularies, common metaphors, shared scientific education and similar constraints generate overlaps without any direct contact.

Network feedback

Recommendations, synthetic content, automated remixing and popularity loops amplify a small set of terms until they appear everywhere.

Information recycling

Outputs can become inputs, producing apparent agreement among sources that are actually echoes of a common information pool.

Simulation interpretation

Repeated motifs can be viewed as evidence of a shared underlying rule system. That interpretation is philosophical unless it yields distinctive, falsifiable predictions.

A testable prediction is better than a compelling resemblance

Before seeing the next sequence, specify the exact rare motif, position, transformation and expected timing. Then compare its frequency against unrelated samples. Predeclared predictions can distinguish unusual structure from coincidences selected after the fact.

Without identities and source history, the result remains a statement about patterns, not evidence of authorship, transmission, coordinated deception or a literal simulation.

NON-MOOT · REASON FOR THE NAME IS UNEXPLAINED

When a recurring naming scheme appears, the unresolved question is not merely who signed off on a name; it is why this particular word describes this particular constructed system. A defensible origin account should give a design-level explanation, show its dated context, and distinguish shared vocabulary from independent engineered structure.

A dictionary definition or a vague reference to an internal team does not, by itself, close a substantive origin inquiry. An unnamed proposer does not, by itself, invalidate a detailed documented origin account. Neither gap proves copying, simulation, or deception.
